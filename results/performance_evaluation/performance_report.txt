3. SCIENTIFIC CORE CONCLUSION: IMPROVEMENT VS. REGRESSION TRADE-OFF
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QUESTION: 'How much does StarveGuard improve or regress system scheduling performance
compared with traditional Normal Priority Scheduling?'

SCIENTIFIC FINDINGS:
1. Throughput & Mean Waiting Time:
   - Normal Priority Scheduling naturally minimizes average waiting time in heterogeneous workloads
     because prioritizing short interactive tasks approximates Shortest Remaining Time First (SRTF).
   - Unconstrained Continuous Aging (K=5) causes a massive +70% to +85% regression in average waiting time
     because long batch jobs elevate to priority 1 and block dozens of subsequent short jobs.
   - TTCA (Conservative Aging with Apex Isolation) completely eliminates this blowout:
     its average waiting time stays within -0.5% to +6% of Normal Priority while actively rescuing
     starved processes.

2. Tail Starvation & Latency Outliers:
   - Continuous Aging achieves the lowest maximum waiting times (reducing extreme tail latency by 10-15 ticks),
     but at severe system-wide throughput cost.
   - Normal Priority permits unbounded starvation when low-priority jobs are starved indefinitely.
   - TTCA bounds low-priority wait times below the starvation ceiling for all processes with base priority > 1
     against intermediate priority competition (priorities 3-10).

3. Simulator Overhead:
   - Preemptive decision logic overhead is well within simulator tolerance (averaging 1-15 microseconds per tick),
     representing negligible simulator-level cost.
